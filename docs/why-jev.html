Why Juardrails · Decision speed

Fast decisions, broad guardrails

A guardrail runs in the path of an action. Jev is built for typed decisions instead of generated prose, and Juardrails turns those answers into an explicit allow, block, review, or error decision.

70–500 msTypeSafe-reported Jev provider response range for its published examples
1 requestJuardrails batches every criterion in a policy into one Jev call
Typed answersChoice, Score, and Noul values feed deterministic policy rules

The timing above is TypeSafe's published provider figure, not a Juardrails end-to-end benchmark. Your result includes network, authorization, provider queueing, policy evaluation, and audit writes. Measure it in your deployment.

Why the decision path can be fast

Jev returns probabilities for structured questions in parallel rather than generating an explanatory answer token by token. Juardrails sends all policy questions with the input state in a single provider request, validates the typed answers, and combines them with all, any, or weighted rules in Go. The caller receives a compact decision it can enforce immediately.

→
One Jev callParallel typed answers for every criterion

Policy decisionThresholds, review gates, and audit

TypeSafe's published workflow evaluations report that decomposed decision workflows are more accurate, faster, and cheaper than asking the tested models to solve the same task in one prompt. Jev sits on a strong accuracy, latency, and cost frontier against the LLM configurations in those tests. These are provider-run evaluations across four automation workflows; they do not establish a speedup or accuracy rate for every Juardrails policy.

Beyond a single threat detector

A purpose-built prompt-injection model can be valuable for that one job. For example, Meta's Prompt Guard model card describes three output labels: benign, injection, and jailbreak. Those labels do not answer whether a proposed shell command is destructive, a tool call exposes a secret, an invoice meets an approval rule, or an agent response violates your own policy.

Juardrails lets you define those questions in YAML and combine them under one policy. You can change the rubric and thresholds without training a new classifier, and use the same service for different applications and agent harnesses.

ApproachWhat it is suited toWhat you must design
Prompt-injection classifierDetecting the attack classes in its model cardAdditional controls for other risks and actions
General LLM judgeOpen-ended interpretation and explanationsPrompting, output parsing, latency and cost controls
Juardrails + JevConfigurable, typed decisions across many policy questionsClear criteria, thresholds, testing, and enforcement by your caller
Flexible scope is not a universal accuracy claim

A specialized detector may outperform a general policy on its own benchmark. Juardrails has no published head-to-head accuracy test against prompt-injection models. Evaluate each policy on representative allowed and disallowed cases, review false positives and false negatives, and tune its thresholds before enforcing it.

Cost and accuracy in practice

Jev's typed outputs and parallel sampling are the basis of TypeSafe's reported cost advantage over LLM-based decision workflows. Its September 2026 analysis describes the workload, comparison models, and important caveats. Juardrails also reduces repeated provider round trips by batching a policy's questions. The final bill depends on your provider, input size, number of calls, and current pricing.

Accuracy depends on the question and policy design. Noul returns a yes probability; Choice and Score provide distributions and confidence. Juardrails can route low-confidence answers to review, while explicit thresholds turn the rest into stable decision rules. That gives teams a way to calibrate for their own risks instead of relying on a single opaque safe/unsafe label.

Measure your own guardrail path

Run live evaluations with representative inputs and record p50 and p95 time from your caller, the returned decision, and provider token usage in juardrails cli history. Measure the whole path with the service in its intended region. Include allowed, blocked, and borderline cases; a fast decision is useful only when its error rate is acceptable for your workflow.

juardrails cli evaluate claude-code-tool-use sample-tool-call.json
juardrails cli history claude-code-tool-use

See policy design for typed criteria and the Claude Code pack for a working multi-risk policy.
